As I wandered through that world, I continued entering other rooms.

There were doors written in the most diverse languages, and others that didn't even seem to belong to any language at all.

None was like the one before.

During one of these strolls, another door caught my attention.

From inside it escaped a warm, vibrant red light.

It was as if a small sun existed solely for that place.

Unlike the other rooms, there was comfort here.

A bed, some furniture, and plants everywhere.

There were vases overflowing with flowers.

Others rested in glass containers, submerged in something I couldn't identify.

There were even those that seemed to sprout directly from the walls.

They grew effortlessly, as if that place had forgotten that seasons existed.

In the center of the room, someone painted in silence.

At first, I thought I was looking at a painting.

Only when I got closer did I realize that the figure itself seemed made of watercolor.

Countless colors slid over one another, forming, for fleeting moments, a human silhouette.

Nothing remained the same for long.

The features changed smoothly.

Sometimes, a pair of glasses appeared on the face.

Other times, it disappeared as if it had never existed.

The eyes also changed color.

Or they simply ceased to exist, dissolving into the shades of paint.

She was coloring a blank canvas using her own fingers.

Each one seemed dipped in a different shade of blue.

I imagined she was painting the sky. Or maybe just a background. It was hard to tell.

There was also a large plant drawn on the canvas.

Still without any tone. Just its outline, taking up almost the entire space, as if waiting patiently for the first color.

I noticed she was talking to other figures scattered around the room.

As I approached, I realized they weren't like her.

They were much more like me.

Perhaps they were people from my own world who, just like me, had found that place.

Even so, there was something different about them.

Each one possessed a color.

Some were almost entirely gray. Others carried shades of purple. There were also some green ones.

None seemed to choose that color. It was as if it simply belonged to them.

"Hi... how are you?"

The figure paused her painting for a moment.

She turned to me and offered a smile as spontaneous as the colors sliding across her body.

"Hi! I'm good." She wiped her fingers together, as if the paints simply vanished. "Welcome to my world."

"Your world is full of life and, most of all, colors. Congratulations on the place!"

Her smile only grew wider.

"That happens when I'm feeling well." She looked around. "The colors end up spreading all over the place."

I observed the canvas for a few moments.

"What are you painting?"

Without hesitating, she pointed one of her blue-tinted fingers at a plant, far from the house. From where I stood, I could only see small pink dots.

"Bougainvillea." She smiled like someone introducing an old friend. "I'm still at the beginning. People usually look at the flower first. I like to start with the sky."

I looked again at the other figures scattered around the room and decided to just ask.

"Can I ask you something?"

She nodded promptly.

"Of course."

"Why do people like me have different colors?"

She wiped her fingers on her own palm, making the blue mix with the red.

"The colors aren't chosen."

I couldn't hide my confusion.

"Then where do they come from?"

She seemed surprised that I still didn't understand.

"From what you offer." She took a brief pause before continuing. "The more you experience this place... the more it learns to see you."

I remembered the numbers I had seen since arriving in that world.

"And those numbers?"

She smiled.

"They are just the way you count them." She turned her eyes back to the canvas. "To us, they are marks. Small fragments of time that you've chosen to leave here. That's why some remain gray. They haven't left anything of themselves here yet."

I nodded.

I believed I had understood. Or, at least, that's what I wanted to believe.

Still, part of me remained confused.

It was then that I noticed a bottle next to her.

It was entirely colorful. Covered in drawings that looked like they had been painted by hand.

It resembled a work of art more than a simple container.

Curiosity got the better of me.

"What is that?"

She picked up the bottle without saying a word. She poured a little of the liquid into her own hand. It was pink. A translucent, almost luminous pink.

"Is it good?"

She brought some of the liquid to her lips and smiled.

"I don't know yet." She looked at the bottle again. "I just created it. I'm still discovering what it will taste like."

She seemed to think for a moment and twirled the bottle between her fingers.

"I would offer you some. But some things only exist for those who belong to this place." She smiled again. "You can see them. You can talk to them. You can even carry them in your memories. But some things can only be experienced by those who belong to this place."

I decided to ask about the rooms.

"How do they work?"

She went back to painting the background of the canvas, spreading different shades of blue with her fingertips.

"We create them." A brushstroke of darker blue crossed the sky. "They are always changing. Just like us."

I continued watching the canvas.

"So... does this place reflect who you are?"

She smiled.

"In a way." She paused briefly before continuing. "The rooms show how you see us. And also how we choose to show ourselves. No canvas is born finished." She spread a little more blue. "The same happens with the rooms. Every conversation changes something. Every memory adds a color. Every visitor leaves a trace."

She observed the canvas for a few moments.

"And, sometimes..." She smiled. "...we discover that we were the ones painted."

Soon other figures approached.

Blue. Purple. Green. Gray.

Some observed the paintings. Others just talked.

The artist seemed to know all of them.

Her smile was the same for every visitor.

I understood that that place would remain alive, long after my departure.
