Upon leaving that room, for the first time, I didn't look for just any door. I decided to look for my own.

I imagined it would be simple. After all... I should recognize the place I belong to.

I didn't. And none seemed to recognize me back.

After getting to know various perspectives, I realized I had never visited or tried to enter my own. I stood still. If all those doors led to the way someone saw the world... where was mine?

I waited. Nothing.

Then I imagined that perhaps it didn't exist yet. I reached my hands into the void, like someone trying to make space for an invisible door. Nothing happened. I walked to an empty space and stood before it. I waited for it to accept me. Nothing changed.

That was when I thought: perhaps it is something we build so slowly that we can only see it after living inside.

Gradually, I began to understand. None of those rooms had appeared out of nowhere. I hadn't yet decided what my shape was.

It was then that I understood what was missing. If I wanted a room... I would need to leave something of myself there.

And, for that, there would be a price: myself.

I would need to accept being seen. To talk. To make mistakes. To be understood by some and misunderstood by others. To let parts of me remain in that place. After all, none of those rooms existed just because someone had created them. They existed because someone had left much of themselves in them.

As I accepted that, I realized I didn't need to find a room. I needed to start letting it exist.

It was strange.

Without realizing it, I started leaving small parts of myself in that place. The songs that always returned to my headphones. Some verses that insisted on remaining in my memory. Thoughts I had never spoken aloud. Memories. Silences. Tastes.

With every small thing I left, the empty space before me seemed to breathe. First, a few centimeters. Then, a little more. It wasn't a door. Not yet. It was just a place that was beginning to recognize me.

I looked at my hands. For the first time, they seemed to occupy a physical space in that world. They were no longer just a presence.

Gradually, a contour began to emerge. It wasn't a complete body. It was just enough so that I ceased to be a formless presence. Perhaps because I was still discovering who I was. But, for the first time, I had a form. Not to hide who I was, but to, finally, be able to present myself.

After that, I managed to open my room a few times.

I would enter. Stay for a few moments. And leave again. In all of them, there was the same feeling: discomfort.

Not because the room was wrong. But because it showed more of me than I was used to showing.

For the first time, it wasn't me observing a perspective. It was my own. I started to wonder if I was really prepared, or if I wanted to allow other people to know it. Perhaps building a room was easier than inhabiting it. Perhaps the real challenge wasn't finding a place to exist, but accepting that people could enter.

Even so, for a brief moment, that room stopped being empty.

The being made of mist appeared once. It entered. It stayed for an instant. Then, it dissipated into the air.

{{mist-being}} Some time later, two gray figures crossed the door. They stayed there for a few minutes. I tried to start a conversation, but neither responded. They didn't even look in my direction. Then, they left.

{{gray-pass}} I spent some time thinking about that. Perhaps they were just passing through. Perhaps they were looking for another room. Or perhaps they had never really entered. Sometimes I wonder if some visitors aren't just memories walking through those corridors.

It has been three weeks since the last time I opened it.

Sometimes I think about returning. But there is a strange balance between the will and the hesitation. I want to enter. At the same time, I prefer not to expose myself.

It’s curious. I spent so much time looking for that room... and now it is I who avoids walking through my own door.

I discovered that finding a perspective was much easier than living inside it.
