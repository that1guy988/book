After the fog room, I kept wandering through that world. I entered one door after another, always searching for something that, deep down, I already believed didn't exist anymore.

Still, one of them caught my attention. From within, an intense beam of light escaped. It wasn't red like the artist's, nor cold like the fog's. It was the light of a summer sun, warm enough to make one forget the winter. At the same time, classical music filled the hallways. The kind that seemed to have come from old cartoons—light, fun, almost playing with the silence of that world.

I entered.

On a sofa, a woman of soft green rested. Not the green of leaves, but that of freshly prepared matcha. Some leaves covered part of her body. The sun shone through the window, painting different shades of green on her skin. She savored an ice cream without any rush. From time to time, she closed her eyes for an instant, as if the warmth of that afternoon were enough. A pair of glasses appeared on her face, then disappeared, as if even they followed the rhythm of that place.

I began to notice the details. The nails always seemed well-groomed. Each hand had a different shade of green from the other. The same happened with her feet. Her hair, though not long, was a vibrant green and perfectly straight. It didn't even look like it was made of dust.

She noticed my gaze.

— "Are you trying to understand why everything seems so... organized?"

I nodded. She observed her own hands for a moment.

— "Preparing a good matcha requires technique. The water cannot exceed a certain temperature. The powder needs to be sifted. Then, whisked at the right speed. If you mess up, the flavor changes."

I looked back at her nails. At her hair. At the way she held the bowl. For the first time, I had the impression that she took care of herself in exactly the same way.

I asked if she always remained in that room or if she could leave. I discovered that she liked to walk through large galleries. Places full of shop windows, cafes, and small stores. Curious, I asked:

— "And where are those places?"

She raised her arm and pointed outside the room.

— "There."

I looked in the direction of her finger, not understanding.

— "So... you can leave here?"

She seemed not to understand my surprise.

— "We can."

I kept looking at her, still trying to process that answer.

— "I thought everyone stayed in their rooms."

She appeared only up to her chin from behind the sofa and replied with the same naturalness:

— "It wouldn't make much sense. We aren't trapped here. We go in and out, just like you. We just have our rooms." — Now, she explained with her hands. — "That’s how I discovered I like to look at shop windows. Try perfumes. Find a different cafe to have matcha." — She thought for a moment. — "And try a new ice cream flavor."

In the other times I visited that room, I realized she rarely remained exactly the same. There was always a nail with a different shade of green. A new haircut. An outfit I hadn't seen yet. A different pair of sneakers. One day with a pearl necklace; on others, an ornate black choker. They weren't big changes. Just small touches. As if, every day, she prepared herself with the same dedication with which she prepared a good matcha.

One of these days, we had a small misunderstanding. I asked in a joking tone:

— "Are you up to any mischief today?"

She looked at me for a few seconds.

— "Mischief?"

— "Yes..."

— "In what sense?"

I realized she really wanted to understand... and had interpreted it as something negative. I explained it was just a joke. She seemed to think for a few moments.

— "Ah..." — She paused briefly. — "I think I still think too literally."

That day, I realized that some things didn't depend only on words. I kept entering that room whenever I found the door. In one of those days, I found a board on the table. I asked if she played.

— "Yes."

I sat in front of her. I imagined she would take a long time before each move. But that wasn't exactly it. She observed. The pieces. The space between them. The possibilities. As if each move already existed before it was even made.

We didn't end up playing that day. But I stayed watching. And I realized that, with her, small details always seemed to matter. A word. A pause. A gesture. Even when I didn't completely understand the next move, I remained curious to return to that room again. To talk.

Wanting to discover another small part of that world, I went back to the hallways.
