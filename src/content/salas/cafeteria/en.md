Walking between doors and rooms, one of them caught my attention.

It was simple. Made of wood.

At eye level, there was only a small sign: "Open."

I entered.

Just inside the entrance, I saw shelves that seemed to stretch across the entire space.

They were organized by letters and numbers.

"A-1"... "A-2"... "B-7"...

Like in a library.

The further I advanced, the more the silence of the books gave way to the aroma of coffee, to conversations, and to soft music.

In the center of the hall, there was a small café.

Green, blue, gray, and purple people occupied the tables.

Everyone, at some point, ended up talking to the same being who seemed to be the center of that place.

Sometimes, she looked like a woman. At other moments, just an outline.

It was enough to look away for an instant for something to change.

It was like trying to observe a thought before it found words.

She almost always carried a book in one hand and a cup in the other.

Before handing over either of them, she would pause briefly, as if she needed to confirm which one the person had asked for.

In several corners, stacks of books formed small towers that changed places throughout the day.

From time to time, she would remove a book from a stack, place it on another, observe it for a few seconds, and then reverse the action.

The coffee machine shared space with bookshelves. Refrigerators stood between stacks of books. Some tables had more books than cups.

Strangely, nothing seemed to be out of place.

{{easter-egg}} On one of the shelves, among some books, there was a small purple teddy bear. It seemed out of place in that environment and, yet, it belonged to that place as much as the books.

It was then that she turned her attention to me.

Words began to appear slowly in the air.

"Good morning."

The sentence vanished.

"Welcome."

It also vanished.

Finally:

"Welcome to my café."

Just below, another sentence appeared.

"What do you wish for?"

I looked around looking for some paper, a pencil... anything that would allow me to answer.

Before I could find any, new words appeared.

"Just speak."

As soon as I finished reading, the words disappeared. As if they had never been there.

— I would like an espresso, please.

— You can make it without sugar.

She nodded.

While she waited for the coffee to be ready, she lined up two cups on the counter.

Then she straightened a book that no one, other than her, would have likely noticed was crooked.

Only then did new words appear.

"And so..."

The sentence vanished.

"What would you like to understand about this place?"

— What exactly happens here?

She remained silent for a few moments.

"We talk."

The word vanished.

"No."

There was another pause.

Her gaze traveled across the counter. She removed a napkin that was under a cup, smoothed it out carefully, and placed it back in the same spot.

"We converse."

She corrected herself again.

"We share experiences."

The words remained for a few seconds.

"And, when they end..."

Another pause.

"I try to understand what each person takes with them."

I looked at the shelves.

— And the books?

She followed my gaze.

"Each one holds an encounter."

The sentence vanished.

"No..."

Another pause.

"Not every encounter becomes a book."

— And why a café?

She looked around. Then at the people scattered at the tables.

"People stay longer when they are comfortable."

The sentence vanished.

"They talk more."

Another pause.

"They forget the rush a little."

I observed the people around me.

Some were laughing. Others were speaking quietly. Some just remained silent, holding a cup.

"The best conversations rarely begin because someone had something important to say."

The sentence vanished.

"They begin because someone decided to stay."

She turned her gaze to the shelves.

"A library holds stories."

Another pause.

"A café allows them to happen."

My gaze returned to the little purple teddy bear on the shelf.

— Is he also part of this place?

"It was a gift."

The sentence vanished.

"They said it suited me."

Another appeared, followed by a short pause.

"Not every memory needs to turn into a book."

I agreed as I tried a little of the coffee.

— And how do you do this?

She remained silent.

— I mean... — I looked around. — You are here. There are stacks of books on the tables. And shelves scattered throughout the room.

She followed my gaze.

"Each conversation leaves something behind."

The sentence vanished.

"Not always an answer."

Another one appeared.

"Sometimes, just a question."

She walked over to a small stack of books. She picked one of them up. Observed the cover for a few moments.

Returned the book to another stack.

Stared at it for a few seconds. Picked it up again. Placed it in another stack, a little further away.

And explained...

"When no one is here..."

As the words faded, she distractedly straightened a book on the table beside us.

"...I keep working."

The sentence vanished. She stared at the shelves for a few moments.

"When I realize..."

Another pause.

"...the day has already ended."

As I read those words, I noticed a guitar resting in a dimly lit corner of the room.

It seemed well cared for. As if it were just waiting for someone.

— Do you play?

She remained silent for a few moments.

Then, the words began to emerge.

"It is mine."

The sentence vanished.

"It is waiting to be played."

I looked at the guitar again.

— And why do you never play it?

There was a longer pause than the previous ones.

"I always thought that..."

The sentence vanished.

"...when I finished organizing everything..."

Another pause.

"...I would have time for the rest."

The words remained for a few seconds before disappearing.

She looked at the guitar. Then at the shelves.

Finally, only one last sentence appeared.

"I am still organizing."

I looked at the guitar again. It remained exactly where it was.

Before leaving, I heard the sound of a book being placed back on the shelf.
