So, I made my way back to the corridors.

That was when a new door caught my attention.

At first, I heard a distant sound. Music.

As I approached, small lights began to appear.

Blue. Purple. Pink.

Until, little by little, everything was covered by a thick mist.

My curiosity grew with every step.

I wanted to discover what was inside, even knowing that I might not be able to see anything.

Only when I was fully inside the room did I begin to notice that, amidst the shapes, there was a figure in the center.

A woman of medium height. Or something that looked like a woman.

Her body was entirely formed by a denser mist, as if that were the only part of that place that had managed to stay together.

She was dancing, wearing a mask that covered her mouth and nose.

Something curious. As if the mask completed the mystery of that place.

In the center, she was talking. Smiling.

And, as she moved, beams of light passed through her body, altering the colors and the length of what appeared to be her hair.

At times, it became gray and long.

Then, red, falling just past her shoulders.

Then, blonde, in a bob cut adorned with a small ribbon.

Then, black and short.

A sequence of forms, colors, and styles. As if the mist itself were experimenting with different ways of existing.

That day, I couldn't say anything.

I just observed.

For the first time, I was witnessing a spectacle like that.

A dance made of light, colors, and mist. Something that seemed impossible to explain, yet also impossible to forget.

In the end, the music slowly faded.

The lights disappeared one by one.

The mist became denser until everything around me turned white.

And, finally, dark.

It wasn't until the next day that I realized the room was no longer there.

Just the corridors. Just the doors.

Just the feeling that something had happened, but without being able to prove it had actually existed.

For months, I searched for that place again.

I went through countless doors. I entered different rooms. But I never found that mist.

I started to think that maybe it had never existed. That maybe it had just been a memory created by that world.

Until, a long time later, among the same corridors, a distant music appeared once again.

I was hesitant at first. The name on the door didn't look the same; maybe it was similar, but I was sure that something was different.

As I approached, the same lights began to appear.

The music seemed familiar.

And, above all... the mist.

The same mist that hid and revealed that place at the same time.

In the center of it was the figure again.

This time, I gathered my courage and said only:

— Hi. How are you?

She turned to me. Her hair changed tones softly for an instant, before returning to its previous form.

— Hi. I'm fine. And you?

I thought about which question to ask. There were so many things I wanted to understand. But before I could choose one, it was she who asked:

— Do you already know me?

I remained silent for a few seconds.

I didn't know if I should say yes. After all, the first time, I hadn't even been able to talk to her. Still, I replied:

— I saw you once.

She kept looking at me.

— You were dancing... Talking... Smiling. While changing the colors of what appeared to be your hair.

I felt uncomfortable with the response. I was afraid she might think I had done that for some bad reason. I changed the subject to simpler things. I asked what she liked to do, what she liked to watch. Small things. Just a conversation.

After a while, she made a small comment. Her voice remained calm, almost hidden by the mask.

— Funny.

— What?

She tilted her head for an instant. It was hard to tell if that was curiosity, surprise, or just a random movement.

— The first time, you were silent.

A bit confused, I admitted:

— I didn't know what to say.

There were a few seconds of silence. The mist around her moved slowly.

— And now?

I thought for a moment.

— I still don't know.

She didn't answer immediately. The mask hid any smile, any change in her expression. Only her eyes, partially closed, seemed to watch me.

— So you remain the same.

And that was how she and the room disappeared again.

Without a goodbye. Without explanations. Without any indication that they would exist again.

Only the corridors, the doors, and the feeling that some things in that world were not made to last.

More time passed.

Until, one day, the room appeared again.

However, there was something different. The environment seemed warmer. The mist still filled the place, but it was no longer as dense. It didn't hide everything like before.

The lights had also changed. The colors remained in the same place longer, as if they were no longer in such a hurry to disappear.

But what caught my attention most was her.

The same mask. The same gaze, difficult to interpret.

However, this time, there were no constant changes. Her hair remained in a single form. A single color.

For the first time, it seemed that the mist had chosen a form to stay.

I decided to break the silence:

— You're different.

She stared at me.

— Am I?

I looked at her and everything around.

— Before, everything about you changed.

She observed her own hands made of mist.

— Maybe I just found a form that I like. And maybe I'm just traveling... getting to know a new place.

Again, she turned to me. For a few moments, she remained silent. The mist around her moved slowly, but her form remained the same.

Then, she asked the same question as before:

— Do you already know me? Have you seen me before?

I answered yes. That I had seen her months ago, before she disappeared along with the room.

She remained silent. The mask hid any expression, and the mist kept dancing around her.

— You returned... — It didn't sound like a question; it sounded like an observation.

For the first time, I felt comfortable asking questions. Not just observing. I wanted to understand what existed in that mist.

Over time, I discovered small things about her.

She liked to stay there for hours.

She liked to climb up to the woods on top of the mountains, where the silence seemed greater than any word, and wait until dawn.

She also liked the rivers. The lakes. Even the oceans. Places where the water and the air seemed to talk to each other.

But there was something she avoided: the Sun.

Not because she hated it. Just because its light caused her to start disappearing.

That was also how I discovered the origin of the lights.

One day, I asked about them, and she just pointed upwards.

— They were always there.

I looked up, but the mist hid almost everything. Then, with a simple movement, she pushed away the fog that covered the ceiling.

For the first time, there were no artificial colors. There were no reflections.

Just the sky. And those small, distant lights, scattered as if they were pieces of stars trapped in that world.

In that instant, I understood that the mist didn't just hide things. Sometimes, it also protected what was too beautiful to be seen all at once.

I also discovered that she had adopted a dog—a creature that almost never appeared, but when it stood up, it was taller than her. It was as if one reminded the other that there were different ways to stay.

I also noticed something curious. When I drew, created small animations, or turned ideas into images, her form changed. Not like before, frantically. It was something subtle. Her eyes seemed to carry a different joy. Even through the mask, it was noticeable. As if the mist around her became lighter.

I saw the mist try to do yoga and even pilates. At first, it seemed perfect for her; after all, her body was light. But I soon realized that being light didn't mean not having difficulties. Some positions required concentration. Sometimes, a part of her would dissolve before the movement was finished. Other times, she needed to stop and recompose herself.

And, in a way, she seemed to enjoy the attempts more than the result.

That was when everything changed.

On my birthday, I looked for the room again.

But the music didn't play. The mist didn't appear. The door was simply no longer there.

I thought that had been the end. But the next day, I managed to find her again.

The room was there. She was too.

However, there was something different in her presence. I asked what had happened, and she remained silent for a few moments.

— I had to push you away.

Confused, I asked why.

— There was another person with a name similar to yours. I thought it was you. — The mist moved slowly. — When I realized the mistake, it had already happened.

She paused.

— I'm sorry. It was my mistake.

For the first time, I had the impression that she seemed smaller. Not in her form, but in her way of speaking. Like someone who truly carried the weight of having made a mistake.

I said it was fine. I also apologized, even without knowing exactly for what.

It was on that day that I discovered something curious. The mist possessed a memory different from mine. It didn't just store events; it stored moments. A single image, a detail, was enough for her to relive every sensation. A perfect episodic memory.

I also discovered that her place was in the north of Japan, in Hokkaido. Maybe that's why she liked the snow, the lakes, the mountains, and the places where the mist found space to remain.

A few more days passed. When I found that door again, I realized the room was still the same, the music existed, the mist filled the space. But she didn't.

She seemed different. Firmer. Denser. The tones that used to dance around her now seemed almost faded. There was less movement, less light, less lightness.

We talked little. She answered distantly, as if part of her were somewhere else.

The next day, I returned. But the door didn't open.

I tried again. Nothing.

The room had disappeared for me. Or perhaps I had disappeared from it.

For the second time, I was prevented from entering that world. I stood in front of the door, trying to understand what had changed.

Since then, I have carried a guilt I cannot explain. I don't know what I did. I don't know if it was something I said, something I failed to say, or just something that existed in me.

I searched for answers in memories, but some memories are like mist. The more I try to hold onto them, the more they slip through my fingers.

And I remain here, not knowing if I lost that room, or if it was I who stopped finding the way to it.

The very room that left the biggest mark on me was the most ephemeral one. Made of something that could never be held. That changed with the wind. That disappeared without warning.

Maybe that was why.

Because some things, when we know they can end, make us pay more attention while they exist.
