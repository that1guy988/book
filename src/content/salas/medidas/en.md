This time, I decided to simply walk through those long corridors. I passed by rooms of all different sizes. Some were full: movement, lights, music, spectacles—they seemed to never rest. Others were almost empty; only their owners remained there, amidst sparse furniture, a touch of light, and a deep silence. Each one seemed to exist at its own pace.

I wasn't looking for anything in particular. I would just open a door, observe for a few moments, and move on to the next. I wanted to get to know that place a little better—and, above all, the people who existed within it.

I came across extraordinary rooms. In one of them, there was a sculpture that, at first glance, reminded me of Polykleitos' works. There was a precision to it that was hard to explain; every proportion felt carefully considered. As I observed it, I noticed the owner of the room walking just behind it. For a moment, the two forms blurred together—not because they were identical, but because they shared the same harmony.

There was a delicacy in the way she held a book, how she walked, how she sat. Even the silence seemed to carry a poise of its own. Nothing felt accidental, as though every movement had found, long ago, its exact measure.

The room itself seemed to follow the same rule: the walls were white; a yellowish light descended from the ceiling while another, reddish one, discreetly warmed one side of the space. The two met without competing for room, creating a welcoming, almost hypnotic atmosphere. Carefully placed plants seemed to form an integral part of that composition, giving me the impression of standing before a place inspired by the ancient architectures of the Middle East and North Africa. Everything seemed to strive for the same goal: balance. Neither excess nor absence—just the feeling that every object occupied the exact spot where it was meant to be.

I stood there for a while, simply observing, before attempting to interact:

"Hello. What are you reading?"

She slowly raised her eyes and smiled:

"El hermafrodita dormido, by Fernando González Ochoa."

I nodded. Unfamiliar with the work, I asked:

"Is it your first time reading something by him?"

She gestured toward a small bookshelf right behind where she was sitting. A few books stood apart from the rest, next to a potted plant and a subtle set of fairy lights that seemed to have forgotten to blink.

"No. I'm also reading Libro de los viajes, by the same author."

"And is there a big difference between them?" I ventured. "I mean... one was written when he was still young; the other, much later."

Before answering, she remained silent for a few seconds. Her gaze drifted among the books scattered around the room.

"Quite a lot," she replied after a brief pause. "One speaks of travels, of the encounters he had with the world. The other... seemed to be an attempt at understanding himself."

She looked back at her book.

"I don't think one is better than the other. They were just written at different moments of the same life."

"And which do you prefer?"

She smiled.

"It depends on the moment I'm living in. I think every book finds us at a different time. Today, I resonate more with this one. Tomorrow..." Her eyes drifted to the stack of books on the floor. "...maybe it'll be the other."

After a moment of thought, she continued:

"It's like The Little Prince. I've read that book at different stages of my life, and in each one, I had a different experience. It was the same book, but I was no longer the same person."

We remained in silence for a few moments. She opened the book once more, and I looked around the room one last time. A few canvases were leaning against the wall. In one of them, there was only water. No fish.

"Do you paint the sea?"

She shook her head.

"Fish. But this time, I'm trying to learn something else."

{{sea-divider}} A few brushstrokes suggested water; others, a sky that hadn't quite decided if it was going to be a sky yet. I asked an almost unnecessary question:

"Are you still painting it?"

She looked at the canvas for a moment:

"I'm still experimenting. Sometimes, I enjoy discovering a texture more than finishing a painting, you know? I add something just to see how it looks. If I like it, it stays. Otherwise... I remove it. Or I simply leave it unfinished. Sometimes I also change the lighting; the same painting can look entirely different."

I stared at the canvas for a moment before asking:

"What makes you keep coming back to the sea?"

She looked at the painting once more.

"Because down there, different rules apply. Other forms of life."

The water occupied almost the entire canvas. I decided to ask:

"And why not the sky?"

She offered a subtle smile.

"I like it too. I can spend hours just looking at it, thinking about nothing at all. The sea is where I stay curious. The sky..." She smiled again. "...is where I rest."

There seemed to be much more to that place than I had managed to discover, but for some reason, it felt like enough. I left the room in silence, yet satisfied. I felt a desire to continue that conversation; perhaps I would return another time, if the room was open.

But for now, curiosity still spoke louder. There were still many doors, many perspectives, and I wanted to know a little more of that place.
