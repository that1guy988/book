As I walked down that corridor, I noticed a door.

Its inscription was written in a language I couldn't identify.

Yet, something about it drew me in.

Upon entering, I saw that everything was yellow.

But instead of sun, there was snow.

The further I ventured, the more astonished I became.

There was no life, there was nothing.

Crumbled buildings scattered across the horizon.

A devastated city, frozen in absolute silence.

In the center of it all, I found a woman made entirely of snow.

She danced around a frozen lake for an audience that did not exist.

With every spin, small flakes detached from her body and vanished into the air.

I drew closer and then noticed a detail.

A massive void occupied the place of her heart.

Yet, she smiled.

Yet, she kept dancing.

As if, with every movement, she refused to surrender what little she had left to the war.

As if there had never been anything to fill that space.

And, even without spectators, she performed without hesitation.

I said nothing.

I just watched.

Her smile seemed incapable of fading.

Around us, the ruins remained silent, buried beneath the yellow snow.

Gradually, I let my guard down.

— Hi.

She paused her dance for a moment.

Her eyes met mine, and her smile remained exactly the same.

— Hello, stranger.

Her voice was soft, almost as light as the flakes detaching from her arms.

— Welcome to my world.

She spun slowly across the ice of the lake.

— A place that was once beautiful.

For a moment, her eyes wandered over the destroyed structures around us.

— Until a war began to devastate everything.

I waited for her expression to change.

For her to show sadness, anger, or regret.

But nothing happened.

She kept smiling.

As if the destruction around her belonged to someone else.

And the massive void in her chest were just another detail.

— And you? — she asked. — Which world did you come from?

— My world is more beautiful and has more colors, but it contrasts with sadness.

She stopped dancing, and the smile remained fixed on her face.

— Is that so?

Her voice was so soft I could barely hear it.

— More colors? — she repeated, as if thinking of something distant.

She looked around the destroyed and frozen city.

— You are looking at the ruins. — She smiled. — Me too.

Her eyes swept over the shattered buildings.

— I just can't look only at them.

— And the sadness?

The smile didn't waver.

— Yes. Sorrows.

She spun again on the ice of the lake, the flakes detaching from her body like tears that would never fall.

— I have my sorrows too.

But she didn't show it. She didn't seem to notice the destruction around her.

— And did you come here to escape those sorrows?

The smile continued to shine, like a light that would never go out.

— Yes. That is what I am doing.

She lowered her head, and her eyes met mine again.

— And you? — she asked again.

— I came because I didn't know what to do in my world; I just wanted to get away for a bit.

The smile seemed to understand.

— Is that so?

She raised her head, and the smile shone even brighter.

I asked how she managed to maintain that smile, that disposition amidst so much contrast.

She just stepped closer... smiled, and went back to dancing.

As time went by, I continued to visit this place.

She was always there.

Sometimes, in the center of the frozen lake.

Other times, among dry vegetation, bent by the cold.

There were days when the snow gave way to rain.

On others, the wind seemed to want to tear from her the last remaining flakes.

Sometimes, when I looked back, she was no longer there.

Shortly after, I would find her dancing in another corner of that frozen world.

That was when I realized that even snow has difficult days.

There were days when her snow seemed solid, as if not even winter could defeat it.

On others, a mere breath of wind was enough to make her look as though she were about to fall apart.

But there was something that never changed.

Her smile.

Her kindness.

The delicacy of every step.

She danced like someone reminding the world itself that war could destroy cities, but it couldn't decide who she would be.

Only then did I understand.

War had already taken the place where she lived, part of who she was, and even the space where a heart should have been.

Even so, she refused to surrender what remained.

Herself.
